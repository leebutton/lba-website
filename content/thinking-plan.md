# Thinking editorial plan

Status: working draft  
Last reviewed: 1 September 2026

## Purpose

The Thinking section should help a prospective client understand how Lee sees difficult work before they make contact. It should:

- demonstrate useful expertise without giving away a generic methodology;
- make Lee's judgement, tone, and way of working tangible;
- create relevant routes into the site from search and professional sharing;
- show that Lee is engaged in a wider conversation by sharing useful work, voices, and ideas from elsewhere;
- give existing contacts a useful reason to return or recommend the site; and
- support a conversation, rather than trying to convert readers through aggressive calls to action.

It should not become a general news feed, an obligation to comment on every NHS announcement, or a collection of generic leadership advice.

## Primary audience

The core reader is a senior person who recognises that progress depends on people outside their direct control. Likely readers include:

- primary care, PCN, federation, and general practice leaders;
- neighbourhood, place, provider, and system leaders;
- ICB commissioners and transformation leaders;
- local government, public health, and public service leaders;
- VCSE leaders and cross-sector partners; and
- people leading service design, organisational development, or improvement work.

## Editorial promise

> Clear, useful thinking for people trying to make progress across organisational boundaries.

Every piece should leave the reader with at least one sharper question, useful distinction, practical action, or new way of seeing the work.

## The three streams

### 1. Making systems move

**Role:** The main practice and authority stream. It shows how Lee thinks about strategy, shared responsibility, alignment, decision-making, and coordinated action.

**Reader's question:** "How do I get important work moving when nobody controls the whole system?"

**Best formats:** Substantial articles, practical frameworks, diagnostic questions, and occasional downloadable tools.

**Proposed first pieces:**

1. **Why shared responsibility does not create shared direction**  
   A cornerstone article explaining why agreement in principle often fails to become coordinated action.
2. **The missing middle between strategy and delivery**  
   A practical account of the choices, ownership, relationships, and operating rhythm that strategy documents often leave unresolved.
3. **When agreement does not hold**  
   Three conditions that help decisions survive beyond the meeting.

**Primary commercial connection:** Strategy and system alignment; facilitation and mobilisation; ongoing advisory support.

### 2. Creating Health

**Role:** The distinctive point-of-view stream. It connects healthcare to place, relationships, public services, voluntary organisations, and the wider conditions that help people live well.

**Reader's question:** "What would change if we designed for health, rather than only delivering healthcare?"

**Best formats:** Essays, conversations, interviews, event reflections, and place-based case studies.

**Proposed first pieces:**

1. **Creating health takes more than healthcare**  
   The defining essay for the stream and the clearest explanation of the idea.
2. **Neighbourhood health is not just a new service model**  
   A timely piece about relationships, place, local knowledge, and the conditions needed for joint work to succeed.
3. **What place changes about how we think about health**  
   An accessible exploration of why context, opportunity, community, and public services matter.

**Primary commercial connection:** Neighbourhood and service development; cross-sector partnership work; Creating Health conversations and events.

### 3. From the work

**Role:** The most immediate and human stream. Short, anonymised observations make Lee's judgement visible without turning client work into case-study marketing.

**Reader's question:** "What are you noticing in real work that might help me this week?"

**Best formats:** Field notes of 400–800 words, short tools, questions to take into a meeting, and lessons from facilitation.

**Proposed first pieces:**

1. **The meeting is not the decision**  
   Why apparent agreement can disappear once people return to their organisations.
2. **A pilot needs an operating model, not just enthusiasm**  
   What promising pilots need if they are to become sustainable services.
3. **When the symptoms are clear but the challenge is not**  
   A short note on resisting premature solutions and framing the real problem.

**Primary commercial connection:** Focused sessions, facilitation, service design, and practical advisory support.

## Recommended launch set

Publish four pieces before the new Thinking presentation goes live:

1. *Why shared responsibility does not create shared direction*
2. *Creating health takes more than healthcare*
3. *The meeting is not the decision*
4. *A pilot needs an operating model, not just enthusiasm*

This gives every homepage stream a real destination, establishes the two major ideas, and makes the field-note stream feel active rather than theoretical.

## Publishing rhythm

- Aim for one substantial piece every four to six weeks.
- Use a shorter field note between substantial pieces when there is something genuinely useful to say.
- Do not promise a fixed public schedule at launch.
- Quality, specificity, and relevance are more important than volume.

## Publishing architecture

The website is the canonical home for Lee's original articles. The email newsletter builds the direct audience relationship, and LinkedIn supports discovery.

```text
Original article on leebutton.co.uk
              ↓
  Buttondown email and curation
              ↓
    LinkedIn excerpt and link
```

### Website

- Keep a small, curated collection of static HTML articles on `leebutton.co.uk`.
- Create `/thinking/index.html` as the editorial landing page.
- Give every original article its own stable URL, metadata, structured data, and social image.
- Introduce a static-site generator only if the collection becomes large enough that maintaining individual HTML pages is burdensome.

### Buttondown

- Use Buttondown for subscriber management, double opt-in, and email delivery.
- Embed the subscription form directly in the website.
- Send from the Lee Button domain once the sending-domain records are configured.
- Keep the website, rather than Buttondown's public archive, as the canonical home of original articles.
- Use a private, subscriber-only, or disabled Buttondown archive initially so it does not become a competing public content library.
- Update the Privacy Policy before launch to explain Buttondown's role in processing subscriber data.

### LinkedIn

- Use LinkedIn as a distribution and conversation channel, not the primary content archive.
- Publish a self-contained excerpt, observation, diagram, or question that gives value before linking to the full article.
- Invite readers back to the website and email list.
- Do not launch a LinkedIn newsletter initially.
- Do not republish full articles there as the default approach.

## Newsletter editorial model

The newsletter should be a curated letter, not merely an alert that a new article exists. Its value is Lee's selection and interpretation: why an idea, article, podcast, project, or conversation is worth the reader's attention.

### Recommended edition shape

1. **A note from Lee**  
   A short opening observation or question connected to current work.
2. **One useful idea**  
   Lee's main article, field note, framework, or practical distinction.
3. **Worth your attention**  
   Three carefully selected articles, podcasts, reports, events, or examples from elsewhere. Each link should include one or two sentences explaining why it matters.
4. **A question to carry**  
   One question readers might take into a meeting, partnership, or piece of work.
5. **Stay in conversation**  
   A quiet invitation to reply, share the edition, read a related piece, or start a conversation.

### Curation standard

- Curate three external items per edition as the default; never add links simply to make the newsletter feel full.
- Explain why each item matters rather than supplying a bare link.
- Include perspectives beyond the NHS where they genuinely illuminate health, place, communities, leadership, or public services.
- Credit the original creator clearly and link to the primary source where possible.
- Distinguish Lee's own interpretation from the source author's claims.
- Avoid becoming a weekly news digest or an indiscriminate list of recommendations.
- Prefer useful, durable material to reactive commentary.

### Relationship between articles and editions

- Not every newsletter needs a new substantial article.
- A newsletter may lead with a shorter original note and strong curation.
- Every substantial website article should be included in an edition.
- Field notes can be published in full in the email while retaining a permanent version on the website.
- External recommendations belong primarily in the newsletter; only enduring resources need to become permanent website content.

## Voice and quality standard

- Calm, direct, generous, and precise.
- British English with the Oxford comma, following the project copy standard.
- Prefer real tensions, decisions, and observations to abstract leadership language.
- Use first person where experience or judgement matters.
- Avoid presenting a single model as universally correct.
- Protect client confidentiality and remove identifying details unless publication is explicitly approved.
- Do not invent metrics, outcomes, quotations, or client circumstances.
- Finish with a useful implication or question, not a forced sales pitch.

## Article structure

Most pieces should use this shape without becoming formulaic:

1. A recognisable tension or situation
2. What is commonly misunderstood
3. Lee's central distinction or point of view
4. A practical framework, example, or set of questions
5. What the reader might do differently
6. A quiet route to a related article or conversation

## Website presentation

### Homepage

Each of the three existing cards should show:

- the stream label;
- the latest or defining article title;
- a one-sentence description;
- a genuine link such as `Read the article`; and
- no `coming soon` or `in development` language once the launch set is ready.

### Thinking landing page

Create a dedicated `/thinking/` page with:

- one featured article;
- a short explanation of the three streams;
- clear stream labels on every article;
- an editorial grid ordered by usefulness and recency;
- a restrained email invitation; and
- no filters until there is enough content to make filtering worthwhile.

### Individual articles

Each article should include:

- title, stream, publication date, and estimated reading time;
- a short standfirst that states the value of reading;
- comfortable long-form typography;
- descriptive page title and meta description;
- canonical URL, social-sharing metadata, and Article structured data;
- links to one or two related pieces; and
- a quiet final invitation to start a conversation.

## Image direction

- Use a consistent editorial graphic system for Making systems move.
- Use place-based photography, commissioned illustration, or restrained abstract imagery for Creating Health.
- Use simple diagrams, annotated notes, or authentic working details for From the work.
- Avoid generic NHS stock photography, staged handshakes, and decorative images unrelated to the article's argument.
- Create all article social images at 1200 × 630 pixels.

## Search and discovery

Search should follow the subject rather than dictate it. Natural topic clusters include:

- neighbourhood health and integrated neighbourhood working;
- primary care strategy and operating models;
- system leadership and partnership working;
- turning strategy into coordinated action;
- service design and improvement; and
- facilitation across organisational boundaries.

The current policy environment makes neighbourhood health, joint working, strategic commissioning, prevention, and place especially relevant. The content should remain useful after the immediate policy cycle rather than merely summarising guidance.

## Confidence and decisions

- **Keep the three existing streams:** 97% confidence. They cover practice, point of view, and lived experience without significant overlap.
- **Lead with Making systems move:** 95% confidence. It is the clearest bridge between reader need and paid work.
- **Use Creating Health as the distinctive long-term platform:** 92% confidence. It differentiates Lee from conventional improvement consultancies.
- **Launch with four real pieces:** 90% confidence. Three is the minimum; four makes From the work feel intentionally active.
- **Publish every four to six weeks rather than weekly:** 88% confidence. This fits the considered brand promise and is more likely to remain sustainable.
- **Build a dedicated Thinking page:** 94% confidence. Once real articles exist, the homepage cards should lead somewhere deeper than an email prompt.
